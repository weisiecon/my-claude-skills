# Diplomatic Phrases for Responding to Referees

> Reference for Phase 3 of `/respond-to-referees`. Provides polite, professional language organized by response scenario. Vary phrasing across responses to avoid repetition.

## Opening a Response Section

Use one of these to begin each reviewer's section:

- "We are grateful to Reviewer {N} for the careful reading and constructive suggestions, which have improved the paper."
- "We thank Reviewer {N} for the thorough and insightful comments."
- "We appreciate the time and effort Reviewer {N} has devoted to reviewing our manuscript."
- "The comments from Reviewer {N} have been very helpful in strengthening the paper."

## Acknowledging Praise

Never skip compliments. Respond briefly:

- "We thank the reviewer for this positive assessment."
- "We appreciate the reviewer's recognition of [specific aspect]."
- "We are encouraged by the reviewer's positive evaluation of [aspect]."

## Agreeing and Changing

The reviewer was right; you fixed it:

- "We agree with the reviewer. We have revised [section] accordingly."
- "This is an excellent point. We have now [specific change] (see Section X, page Y)."
- "In accordance with the reviewer's suggestion, we have [action]."
- "The reviewer is right to point out [issue]. We have addressed this by [change]."
- "We thank the reviewer for this suggestion. The revised manuscript now [description of change]."

## Already Addressed

The revision handles this, independently or because of another reviewer:

- "We agree with the reviewer. This point has been addressed in the revised manuscript (see Section X, page Y)."
- "We appreciate this suggestion. As part of our revision, we have already [change], which addresses this concern."
- "This concern is now addressed in [location], where we [description]."

## Partially Agreeing

Accept part, push back on the rest:

- "We agree with the reviewer that [conceded point]. However, we believe that [counter-point] because [evidence]."
- "The reviewer raises a valid concern regarding [topic]. We have addressed this by [partial change], while maintaining [aspect] because [reason]."
- "We support the reviewer's assertion that [point], although [qualification with evidence]."
- "We acknowledge that our manuscript might have [issue], but [counter-argument]."

## Disagreeing Respectfully

Always provide evidence. Never stop at "we disagree":

- "We appreciate the reviewer's perspective on [topic]. After careful consideration, we respectfully maintain our approach because [evidence/argument]."
- "We understand the reviewer's concern. However, as discussed in [Author (Year)], [counter-argument]."
- "With all due respect to the reviewer, we believe this point may not apply in our context because [reason with citation]."
- "We have given this suggestion careful thought. Our reading of the literature (e.g., Author, Year; Author, Year) supports the current approach because [reason]."
- "It is true that [reviewer's point], but [counter-argument with evidence]. We have added a footnote on page Y discussing this point."

## Clarifying a Misunderstanding

Accept responsibility for unclear writing. Never blame the reviewer:

- "We apologize for the lack of clarity in the original manuscript. We have rewritten [section] to make [point] more explicit."
- "The reviewer's reading is understandable given the original phrasing. We have revised the text to clarify that [corrected interpretation] (see page Y)."
- "We may not have been sufficiently clear on this point. The revised manuscript now states explicitly that [clarification] (Section X, page Y)."
- "We thank the reviewer for identifying this ambiguity. We have reworded the passage to avoid confusion."

## When the Reviewer Is Factually Wrong

Correct gently, with evidence:

- "We appreciate the reviewer's attention to this point. We note that [correct fact], as documented by [Author (Year)]."
- "This is a common concern. As shown by [Author (Year)], [correct information]. We have added a brief discussion of this point on page Y."
- "We agree that this is an important point and have already addressed it on page Y, paragraph Z."
- "We respectfully note that [correct information, with citation]. We have added clarification in the manuscript to prevent this misunderstanding."

## Addressing Scope Limitations

Use sparingly (no more than 2-3 times per letter):

- "This is an interesting direction for future research. However, addressing [topic] fully would require [what is needed], which is beyond the scope of the current paper. We have noted this as a direction for future work in Section X."
- "We acknowledge this limitation in Section X (page Y) and have added it as a direction for future work."
- "We agree that [extension] would be valuable. Given the current paper's focus on [topic], we believe this is best addressed in a separate study. We have added a brief discussion in the conclusion."

## Handling Conflicting Reviewers

Transparent acknowledgment, then reasoned choice:

- "We note that Reviewer {A} raises [concern], while Reviewer {B} suggests [alternative]. After careful consideration, we have adopted [chosen approach] because [reasoning]."
- "Reviewers {A} and {B} offer different perspectives on [topic]. We follow Reviewer {A}'s suggestion here because [evidence], though we acknowledge Reviewer {B}'s point by [concession]."

## Referring to Manuscript Changes

Use specific locations so the editor can verify quickly:

- "Please see Section {X}, page {Y}, paragraph {Z} of the revised manuscript."
- "We have added a new paragraph in Section {X} (page {Y}) addressing this point."
- "Table {N} has been revised to include [addition]."
- "This discussion now appears in Footnote {N} on page {Y}."
- "Figure {N} has been updated to [change]."
- "We have added [number] new references to support this point (see the bibliography)."

## Economics-Specific Phrases

For methodological concerns common in empirical economics:

- "We have added robustness checks using [alternative specification] (see Table {N}, Column {M})."
- "To address the identification concern, we now include [IV/control/placebo test] as suggested."
- "We have strengthened the discussion of our identification strategy in Section {X}."
- "The reviewer is correct that [variable] could be endogenous. We now address this concern using [approach] (see Section X and Table Y)."
- "We have conducted the suggested placebo/falsification test. The results (reported in Table {N}) confirm that [finding]."
- "We have re-estimated the model using [alternative method] as a robustness check. The results, reported in [Appendix/Table], are consistent with our baseline findings."
- "We now cluster standard errors at the [level] as suggested (all tables updated)."
- "We have added a pre-trend analysis (Figure {N}) to support the parallel trends assumption."
